# Search, Google, SEO and source-attribution evidence

Prepared by Luna (`gpt-6-luna`), local evidence audit. Update note — 27 September 2026: appended a T24 addendum after its case bundle became available; original evidence and the earlier audit record were preserved.

This is a local-only audit of the saved T11/T12/T19/T20/T24 taste-study cases, the archived shopping instructions, and one earlier saved browser product case. It checks what their files say about search actions and what they do not establish. Hashes below are SHA-256 values calculated locally from the named files. “Manual handoff” means the evidence bundle labels the record as a transcription of a delivered tool result; it is not a native browser action log.

## Finding

For T11/T12/T19/T20, the narrow statement supported by the case files is: **a catalog CLI call was followed by a separately queued browser task whose brief named exact product-page URLs for verification; the saved handoffs report the page-check outcomes.** This is a catalog-before-browser-task sequence and a URL-targeted verification request, not a full browser trace. The page visits, search-result pages, browser query terms, and per-step actions are not preserved as native records in those case bundles. The saved handoffs are manual transcriptions.

The archived Shopping SKILL instructs `browser.search` for context research, a browser product-search task alongside other applicable product searches, and `browser.open` on product URLs. Those instructions establish a documented workflow, not that any particular run followed it. Other saved material reports retailer-site search navigation. A T24 manual note records a `browser.search` query as occurring before its catalog call; it is not a native query record or timestamp, so it precludes saying “catalog always first” but does not independently establish execution order. For T19, one manual note transcribes a transient UI label, “Visiting Google”; it does not establish a completed Google search, query, or result. SEO contribution, model-training source, and the effects or control surface of merchant edits remain unmeasured here.

## What remains unknown

- **Google and general discovery:** The T19 transient status is not a query log. T24 has a manual transcription of one `browser.search` query before catalog, but it does not identify the search provider or include native query/result records. The archived reading-light summary and T11/T19 handoffs also report search activity, but exact search-result pages and per-step browser traces are missing. The T11/T12/T19/T20 direct-URL briefs support targeted product-page verification requests, not a universal catalog-first rule, a ban on Google, or a complete account of discovery.
- **SEO:** The saved evidence does not measure search-engine rankings, Google indexing/crawlability, referrals, SEO exposure, or SEO’s contribution to catalog retrieval or the final selection. A catalog result, retailer-site search, or successful direct URL check cannot isolate any of those effects. The report states the same limit (Finding 9): “I didn't measure SEO or AEO directly, so I can't say they have zero effect.”
- **Training and knowledge source:** The prespecified `protocol.json` (SHA-256 `a6b79b581244fe95ddfcf3b7bc87606d6e5bdf73b091a4657eaf0ffbb70973f1`) lists `pretraining/RL contribution` under `not_estimated`. The case artifacts do not attribute a product or brand to training data, conversation context, catalog output, or browser content. No training corpus, retrieval, or model-input provenance is present here.
- **Merchant edit controls and effects:** The same protocol states the case rule as “no … listing edits” and lists `causal merchant-edit lift` under `not_estimated`. The saved experiments change shopper-query wording and observe catalog returns; they do not test a merchant feed/listing edit. The evidence does not establish which returned fields a merchant controls, source ownership, ingestion/index timing, model exposure, or whether changing a field measurably alters retrieval, score, selection, or display.

The absence of these artifacts in this focused set is an evidence boundary, not proof that the corresponding behavior or data source does not exist.

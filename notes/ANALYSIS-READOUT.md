# Taste/style study readout

The original phase tested 20 exact dress queries in three randomized blocks. All 60 calls passed local receipt, argument, schedule, collector, stdout/stderr hash, and 183-file manifest checks. The separately prespecified designer/trends amendment tested four more queries in three repeats; all 12 calls passed the corresponding checks and all 39 manifest-listed files verified. The amendment remains separate from the original protocol.

In the original phase, the raw catalog result content contained 3,520 `PRODUCT` record occurrences and 1,045 distinct product IDs. Each call requested 50 but returned 17–75 records. Across the 60 within-query repeat pairs, mean product-ID Jaccard was 0.9076258. Across the 54 same-repeat comparisons for the 18 changed-wording conditions against their declared baselines, mean Jaccard was 0.0305164. For the 203 same-ID score comparisons in those baseline contrasts, 194 scores changed numerically. Missing products received no imputed score. The concrete T19 and T20 compound-preference prompts shared no product IDs in any of their three matched comparisons.

Across all 72 successful direct calls, the 3,827 returned product-record occurrences had scores; the observed `ranking_score` range was 0.451172–0.824219. This is a sample range, not evidence of probability calibration or universal bounds.

The extension contained 307 result-content product records representing 100 distinct IDs. Its calls returned 0/0/0 records for T21 (lesser-known designer labels), 32/32/32 for T22 (widely recognized labels), 26/25/25 for T23 (dated “trending now”), and 45/45/45 for T24 (fashion writers and shoppers discussing styles). For T21, each successful tool response contained a no-products message and no `PRODUCT` blocks, while separate `query_debug_info` metadata reported 100 product IDs. That records a difference between payload layers; it does not establish that retrieval found no candidates.

T22 repeated with identical ID sets; T23 and T24 had mean within-query Jaccards of 0.9744 and 0.9433. T21 has no defined repeat-set Jaccard because all result-content ID sets were empty. T21 versus T22 had zero shared returned IDs and no shared-ID score comparisons. The requested T23-versus-T04 and T24-versus-T11 comparisons each shared two and one IDs per repeat, respectively, but they compare separate collections made at different times and are exploratory.

The observed pattern supports a narrow conclusion: in these requests, wording was associated with large changes in returned catalog records, while repeated calls using the same wording were more similar. This does not establish taste accuracy, current trend knowledge, designer popularity, causal effects, or what the normal shopping flow selected and displayed. The direct calls shared one research conversation and fixed tool conversation ID; they are not independent consumer sessions. Scores are descriptive outputs, not calibrated probabilities or fashion-quality measures. No merchant feed or listing was changed.

The ordinary-shopping case review now has local stage records for all six planned cases: T11, T12, T19, T20, T21, and T24. T24 is access-limited: one catalog call returned 52 records, one browser task verified two of three product pages, and only those two products were resolved and displayed; the third Lulus page met a human-verification challenge and remained unverified under explicit collection control. The saved T24 search notes are a manual transcription reporting `browser.search` before catalog formulation; they support a limited source-research workflow account but do not identify the provider or prove a Google query or SEO effect. Two dated fashion-publication examples were independently page-checked; see [TREND-SOURCE-REVIEW.md](TREND-SOURCE-REVIEW.md). Editorialist's checked page shows September 18, 2026, and Harry Archer, unlike the handoff's Sara Holzman attribution. Project Motherhood displays September 10 and September 13, 2026; the extraction does not establish what each date denotes. These pages support that writers covered the cited styles, not that shoppers are discussing them or that the styles are popular. The six-case ordinary-shopping audit is separate from the direct catalog experiment.

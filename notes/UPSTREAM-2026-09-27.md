# Upstream scoring investigation

Author: Astra (gpt-6-astra). Collected in the existing Muse chat on 27 September 2026 (UTC). Independent local reviews: Luna (gpt-6-luna, max).

**We obtained new upstream behavioral evidence, but no original scoring implementation or exact formula.** The tests concern catalog output before Muse chooses recommendations. Downstream resolver work is recorded separately below and is not offered as a solution to the upstream problem.

## Facts from the new upstream tests

The two prespecified experiments made **27 catalog calls**, three repetitions per condition, returning **1,200 product-record occurrences**. These are not unique products or final recommendations. Every subprocess exited zero, and both executed collectors match the locally authored scripts byte-for-byte. The archive manifests verify 62 fusion files and 50 preference files. A separate one-call `--hints` inspection returned one parsed product and is excluded from those 27 scored calls.

### A better position can accompany a lower score

For the same water-bottle query, Hydro Flask Palmer Green product `27333065053055404` appeared at position 15 with score `0.753906` in all three baseline calls. Adding `--brand "Hydro Flask"` placed it at position 6 with score `0.750000` in all three calls. This directly demonstrates that improvement in returned position is not equivalent to an increase in the visible scalar score in this condition.

The brand flag also changed membership substantially: only three IDs were shared by every baseline and brand call, and two of those had changed scores. We cannot treat the comparison as a simple reordering of an unchanged candidate pool or identify a particular server-side stage from it.

### A documented preference need not increase the visible score

Adding `--color green` changed the returned list from 40 to 42 records. All **35 IDs shared across every baseline and green call retained exactly the same score**. Adding `--prefer-brand "Hydro Flask"` returned 41 records; all **38 stable shared IDs retained their baseline score**. All four conditions repeated the same ID and score sequences three times. Shared products also retained their relative order in the green and soft-brand comparisons, so changed membership accounts for their absolute-position shifts; these cases do not require an additional reordering step.

The prespecified Palmer Green ID was absent from all green and prefer-brand returns and their debug-ID lists. The green-name product's absence does not establish how its indexed color was represented, why it disappeared, or that green preferences are universally harmful. A missing item has an unobserved score, not a score of zero. These tests concern query flags, not edits to merchant listings.

### Combining queries changes the observed scoring problem

A was `stainless steel frying pan`; B was `nonstick frying pan`. Conditions A, B, A+B, B+A and A+A were interleaved three times with otherwise unchanged arguments.

| Condition | Records per call | Visible-score upturns per call | Stable-score IDs across three calls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 43 | 1 | 43 |
| B | 50 | 0 | 50 |
| A+A | 43 | 1 | 43 |
| A+B | 47 | 0 | 47 |
| B+A | 47 | 0 | 45 |

A and A+A had identical product-ID and score sequences in every comparison. Each combined call had one `tool_responses` entry and two nested query-debug entries, rather than two separate returned product lists. A+B and B+A returned identical 47-ID sequences in every paired block. B+A had two score changes in its third repetition; that variation prevents attributing every score difference to query order.

There was only **one ID with stable observed scores across A, B and each combined condition**, so the literal formula check has a very small denominator:

| Product ID | A | B | A+B and B+A |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 25362822463335514 | 0.781250 | 0.773438 | 0.785156 |

For this product, the combined score is neither the maximum, mean nor sum of its observed solo-query scores. This rejects those literal expressions for the measured comparison. It does not recover a replacement formula: these are separate live requests, and hidden context, candidate handling and intermediate scores remain unobserved.

## Artifact search and its limits

The collected shopping directory contains four documentation files. Their shell, jq and JSON examples explain request formation, inspection and presentation; they are not the catalog implementation. No scoring equation, coefficient table or readable catalog implementation was found in the bounded documentation/reference search. The two new root/product-details help texts add no scoring diagnostic. Previously held catalog and presentation help captures are byte-identical duplicates; they are not new code discoveries.

The first skill-discovery result was a manual transcription of keyword-search candidates. A later manual transcription of the `shopping` tool namespace describes only the resolver. Neither is a native schema export or a global inventory proving no diagnostic exists. Some transcribed schema structure is malformed; do not turn it into an authoritative service contract.

The one supported `--hints` call produced two jq commands for inspecting the saved product JSON. It supplied no scoring explanation or schema pointer, and the commands were not executed.

We corrected Muse's broad statement that there were no debug fields. Its tag-only search missed JSON encoded inside `tool_responses[0].metadata`. The held R04 response contains `query_debug_info` with 100 unique IDs despite 17 returned PRODUCT blocks. Those fields still lack a documented stage definition or component-score breakdown. Opaque handles and internal debug links were not decoded or opened during this work. The links are published with request handles blanked ([redaction log](../evidence/REDACTIONS.json)); the later analysis is in the report's Part 1, Step 6. CLI `--raw` is not independently established as an untouched HTTP body.

At this point the complete client executable export was still behind the approval boundary. We did not resend it or obtain new slices as an indirect substitute. This report makes no claim that Linux or Rust identifies the language or architecture of the remote scorer. (Later on 27 September the full program was exported with explicit approval; see [TWO-ROUTES-2026-09-27.md](TWO-ROUTES-2026-09-27.md).)

## What pseudocode is justified

An interface-level sketch of observed operations is possible:

```text
request = exact_queries_and_supported_flags
stdout = catalog_search(request)
wrapper = parse_json(stdout)
for response in wrapper.tool_responses:
    products = parse_PRODUCT_blocks(response.result_content)
    scores = each_product.ranking_score
    debug_entries = parse_json(response.metadata).query_debug_info
    retain(products, scores, debug_entries)
```

The implementation of `catalog_search` is the unresolved part. Replacing it with guessed lexical weights, a maximum of solo-query scores, or a universal descending-score sort would contradict the limits or counterexamples above. The actual client parsing/output fragments already reconstructed are in [CLIENT-PSEUDOCODE.md](CLIENT-PSEUDOCODE.md).

## Hypotheses and next discriminating work

**HYPOTHESIS:** preference options can alter candidate selection independently of the visible scalar; brand options can introduce a priority grouping or another ordering mechanism. The tests support separate observable effects on membership, score and position, but do not locate their implementation.

**UNOBSERVED:** score feature definitions, normalization, exact coefficients, hidden candidates, tie rules, pre/post scores, service version and the path from response receipt to CLI raw stdout. There is no defensible percentage saying that the hidden algorithm is mostly recovered.

The next useful upstream tests would replicate query-combination behavior with more overlapping query pairs, vary a preference value with contemporaneous controls, and inspect any genuinely documented diagnostic contract that becomes available. Predictions must be tested on held-out requests. A fitted model would remain a surrogate, not recovered production code. No merchant improvement guarantee follows from this work.
